# Project Compiler — 3:30 Demo Script

Read **Section 1** first. The whole demo happens in **one account (the demo account)** using **two ready-made projects**. You never create an account or a project on stage.

---

## 1. What you'll be using

**Account:** the demo account. On the sign-in page, click **Explore the demo project**; there's no typing. (If you ever need them: `demo@projectcompiler.local` / `demo-password`.)

**Projects** (both already exist in the demo account):

| | Project | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **B** | "Detecting Fake News in Nigerian Pidgin on WhatsApp" | A research project, with flaws an examiner would catch |
| **C** | "Solar-Powered Automatic Irrigation Controller for Smallholder Farms" | A hardware (engineering-build) project, with its own step-by-step path |

**Moving around:** the **Project Compiler** logo (top left) always goes back to the list of projects. Inside a project, the **left sidebar** lists its steps; each step is one page.

**Why prepare beforehand:** the AI takes 15–20 seconds per action. With only 3:30, you run those actions **before** the demo (Section 2), and on stage you just open the results.

---

## 2. Prepare (30–60 minutes before)

Do these in order, on the live site.

1. Open the live site in **Chrome or Edge**. Click **Explore the demo project**.
2. **Project B, run the check:** open *Detecting Fake News…* → sidebar **Check your project** → tick **Include AI reasoning** → click **Run project check** (or **Re-run check**). Wait about 20 seconds. Confirm the top issue is **Critical** and mentions **regression** vs **classification**. If it doesn't, run it once more.
3. **Project B, prepare one defense answer:** sidebar **Rehearse your defense** → **Generate questions** (wait about 15 seconds) → click a question marked **high risk** about the method → type this weak answer:
   > I chose a regression model because it gives a credibility score. It performed well with an F1 of 0.81, and the system is ready for national deployment.

   → **Submit answer** (wait about 10 seconds). Leave it there.
4. **Project C, run the check:** Project Compiler logo → *Solar-Powered Automatic Irrigation…* → **Check your project** → tick **Include AI reasoning** → **Run project check**. Wait about 20 seconds.
5. **Project C, set up the live moment:** sidebar **Define requirements** → find the row **"Detect dry soil"** → make sure its **Target** and **Unit** boxes are **empty** (clear them if not). The tile should read **Measurable 4/5**.
6. **Sign out** (bottom of the sidebar), so the sign-in page is showing. Set zoom to **110–125%**. Turn off notifications.

Also record a **backup video** of one full run, in case the Wi-Fi fails.

## 3. The script (3:30)

### 0:00–0:20: the problem (sign-in page)

- Point at the coloured chain on the left (*Problem → Gap → Objectives → Method ⚠ → … → Defense*).
- **Say:** "Final-year students treat their project as a document. Examiners treat it as a system: this chain. One broken link, and it shows up at the defense. We don't write the project. We make sure the student can defend it."
- Click **Explore the demo project**.

### 0:20–0:40: the guided path (Project B)

- Click **Detecting Fake News in Nigerian Pidgin on WhatsApp**. You land on its **Home**.
- Point at the **path** of big circles and the purple card's button.
- **Say:** "Every student gets a guided path, like a learning app: one next step, one button. Each step has a checklist that ticks itself off from the student's real work."

### 0:40–1:30: it catches what an examiner would (Project B)

- Sidebar → **Check your project**. The results are already there, because you ran the check in Section 2.
- Click the top **Critical** issue to open it.
- **Say:** "The problem statement says classification. The method says regression. A panel would catch this in five minutes. We caught it, explained why it matters, pointed at the exact parts, and said what to do next."
- Point quickly at the **health score**, and at one more issue (the source that **challenges the research gap**).
- **Say:** "Rules check the structure instantly; AI judges the meaning. And nothing the AI suggests goes into the project unless the student accepts it."

### 1:30–1:55: the defense rehearsal (Project B)

- Sidebar → **Rehearse your defense**. Click the question you answered in Section 2.
- Point at the scores and the red **Contradicts your project** / **Unsupported claims** sections.
- **Say:** "Questions come from the project's own weak spots. This vague answer claims national deployment and a regression model. The examiner caught both, and lists the follow-ups a real panel would ask."

### 1:55–3:10: hardware projects (Project C)

- Project Compiler logo → **Solar-Powered Automatic Irrigation Controller for Smallholder Farms**.
- **Say:** "Engineering students build things, so hardware projects follow the path engineering departments use: requirements, design, build, test."
- Sidebar → **Define requirements**. Point at **Measurable 4/5**.
- In the **"Detect dry soil"** row: **Target** → type **≤ 30**, **Unit** → type **% moisture**, press **Enter**.
  - The checklist completes and a **"Step 2 complete!"** window appears with confetti.
  - **Say:** "Every requirement must be measurable. One wasn't. Now it is, and the step completes. Small wins keep students moving."
- Click **Stay here**, then sidebar → **Build & test**.
- Point at the **Traceability matrix** and the red **fail** on the battery test.
- **Say:** "Every requirement is traced to its design and a measured test. The battery lasted 5.5 hours against a target of 8. Flagged, not hidden."
- Sidebar → **Check your project** (results already there). Point at an issue about the **relay** rating, or at a **"has no test"** critical.
- **Say:** "And the AI spots what a lecturer would: a relay rated 2 amps driving a 3-amp pump."

### 3:10–3:30: close

- Sidebar → **Home**.
- **Say:** "Idea, evidence, design, testing, defense, for research and engineering projects. The student does the work. Project Compiler makes sure they can defend it."

### Bonus (only if you're ahead of time, or during questions): start a project from scratch (~45 s)

Do this **only if you reach the hardware section before 2:30**, or when a judge asks "how does a student start?". Insert it just before the close. It creates a **new project inside the demo account**, not a new account.

1. Click the **Project Compiler** logo → **+ New project** (top right).
2. Leave **Research, software or study** selected.
3. In **"What is your project about?"**, paste:
   > I want to study why smallholder farmers in Kano are not adopting solar-powered irrigation pumps even though they are cheaper in the long run.
4. In **Discipline**, type **Agricultural Economics**. **Leave "Working title" empty.**
5. Click **Create & analyse my idea**.
   - While it loads (~15 s), **say:** "A student starts with nothing but a rough idea, not even a title. Watch what it does with it."
6. When the results appear, point at three things:
   - **The title in the left sidebar.** It was "Untitled project"; now it's a proper academic title (e.g. *"Determinants of Solar-Powered Irrigation Pump Adoption among Smallholder Farmers in Kano"*). **Say:** "It suggested an academic working title."
   - **The small labels** beside each point of the brief ("AI inference", "AI suggestion", "Unknown"). **Say:** "It separates what the student said from what the AI inferred, and admits what's unknown. It never passes a guess off as fact."
   - **The questions** it asks. **Say:** "And it asks the hard questions a supervisor would, before the student has invested months."

Afterwards, delete this project (see Section 6) so it doesn't clutter the demo account.

If the analysis takes longer than 20 seconds, don't wait: say "it's thinking about the idea", click the **Project Compiler** logo, and go to the close.

## 4. If something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| No celebration window | Someone had already filled in that target. Don't stop; point at the ticked checklist and move on. |
| The check results look different from this script | The AI words things differently each time. Point at whichever **Critical** issue is at the top. |
| A page shows an AI error | Say "the rule checks don't depend on the AI" and point at the rule-based issues (they're marked **rule**). |
| Project B or C is missing | See "Resetting" below. For now, play the backup video. |
| The audience sees a Vercel login page | They're on the wrong address. Share the site's main address, or turn off Vercel → Settings → Deployment Protection. |
| No internet | Play the backup video and narrate with this script. |

## 5. Likely questions (after the demo)

| Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- |
| Isn't this just ChatGPT? | No. The project is a set of linked parts checked by fixed rules. The AI only makes judgement calls, points at the exact parts, and nothing it suggests is added unless the student accepts it. |
| Does it write the project? | No. It checks and structures the student's own work; the report even marks what's still `[MISSING]`. |
| Why a separate hardware path? | Engineering projects are judged on whether every requirement is measurable, met by the design and proven by a test. That's a different journey from a research project. |
| Does it work outside computer science? | Yes: the demo covers computer science and electrical engineering, and it adapts to other fields such as agricultural economics. |
| Can we try it? | Yes. Click **Explore the demo project** on the sign-in page, or create an account and start from your own idea. |

---

## 6. Resetting after a rehearsal

A rehearsal changes the demo. Before the real presentation:

1. **Project C:** **Define requirements** → "Detect dry soil" → clear the **Target** and **Unit** boxes.
2. **If you tried the bonus:** open the project you created → **Home** → **Delete project** (right-hand panel).
3. That's all. The check results and the defense answer can stay; they're what you want on screen.

If Projects B or C have been deleted or badly changed, on the computer with the project code run `npm run seed` (recreates both in the demo account), then redo Section 2.
